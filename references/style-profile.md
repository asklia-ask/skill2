# Learned Style Profile

Use this profile when no newer user-supplied reference set supersedes it. It records the stable traits demonstrated by the local examples and successful Eiffel Tower, lighthouse, arch bridge, and Nanpu Bridge tests.

## Stable visual language

- A collectible architectural maquette photographed like a restrained museum-shop object.
- Warm ivory or cream seamless paper background with large quiet negative space.
- Soft, broad, diffuse light; gentle tonal gradient; shallow contact shadow rather than a dramatic cast shadow.
- Slight three-quarter product view when supported by the source; front or elevated view for bridges when it better preserves topology.
- Refined, physically plausible material treatment: aged bronze or dark metal for exposed iron lattice; warm off-white, plaster, stone, or matte resin for masonry and pale engineering models.
- Fine but legible construction detail. Repetition is simplified consistently rather than made noisy or ornamental.
- Complete object, centered, no display caption, plaque, watermark, city diorama, people, vegetation, vehicles, water, or scenic base.

## Geometry boundary

Style references control material, lighting, background, scale feeling, and degree of detail. They never control the target's silhouette, bridge type, leg count, tower count, openings, platforms, cable system, or attached structures. Source geometry wins whenever style and identity conflict.

## Common failures to reject

- A scenic city miniature instead of one isolated landmark.
- Glossy toy plastic, chunky blocks, cartoon proportions, or fantasy decoration.
- Over-regularized lattice or masonry that changes the landmark's structure.
- Strong vignette, hard spotlight, dramatic colored light, or cluttered pedestal.
- Text, labels, plaques, logos, or watermarks introduced by the model.
